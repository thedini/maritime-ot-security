# Phase 4: Final Presentation & Demonstration

## Overview

Phase 4 is the culmination of your semester-long capstone project. Your team will present your comprehensive security solution for the M/V CyberSea to a panel of stakeholders, demonstrate key technical components, and defend your design decisions. This phase tests your ability to communicate complex technical concepts to diverse audiences and showcase the practical value of your work.

**Duration**: Weeks 13-14 (Finals Period)
**Deliverable**: Final Presentation (slides + demo) + Integrated Final Report
**Presentation Date**: Week 14 (scheduled during finals period)
**Weight**: 25% of total project grade

## Learning Objectives

By completing Phase 4, your team will:

- **Communicate** complex security architectures to technical and non-technical audiences
- **Demonstrate** working security controls through live or simulated examples
- **Defend** design decisions under questioning from stakeholders
- **Synthesize** semester-long work into cohesive narrative
- **Present** with confidence and professionalism
- **Respond** to feedback and challenging questions
- **Showcase** practical cybersecurity skills

## Final Presentation Requirements

### Presentation Structure

Your 20-minute presentation should follow this structure:

#### 1. Introduction (2 minutes)
- **Team introduction**: Names, roles
- **Project overview**: M/V CyberSea security architecture design
- **Presentation roadmap**: What you'll cover

#### 2. Problem Statement & Context (3 minutes)
- **Maritime cybersecurity landscape**: Why this matters
- **M/V CyberSea profile**: Brief vessel description
- **Security challenges**: Key threats and vulnerabilities identified
- **Project objectives**: What you set out to achieve

#### 3. Assessment Findings (3 minutes)
- **Asset inventory summary**: Critical systems identified (Phase 1)
- **Threat landscape**: Top threat actors and scenarios (Phase 1)
- **Vulnerability findings**: Key vulnerabilities discovered (Phase 2)
- **Risk assessment**: Overall risk posture before your architecture
- **Compliance gaps**: Standards compliance before intervention

**Tip**: Use visuals—show network diagram with threats mapped, vulnerability heatmap, risk matrix

#### 4. Defense Architecture (8 minutes) - **Core Content**
This is the heart of your presentation. Cover:

**Network Segmentation** (2 minutes):
- Show before/after network diagrams
- Highlight zone isolation and trust boundaries
- Explain firewall placement and rule philosophy
- Demonstrate risk reduction through segmentation

**Intrusion Detection & Monitoring** (2 minutes):
- IDS/IPS deployment strategy
- SIEM architecture
- Alert correlation and response workflow
- Show sample dashboard or alert (demo preview)

**Security Controls** (2 minutes):
- Endpoint protection (whitelisting, AV, hardening)
- Access controls (authentication, authorization)
- Operational controls (patch management, training)
- Physical security integration

**Incident Response** (2 minutes):
- IR framework overview
- Role assignments
- Sample playbook walkthrough (pick one: ransomware or GPS spoofing)
- Communication and escalation procedures

#### 5. Implementation & Cost (2 minutes)
- **Phased roadmap**: 12-month implementation timeline (Gantt chart visual)
- **Budget breakdown**: $500K allocation across phases
- **ROI analysis**: Risk reduction, cost avoidance, compliance benefits
- **Success metrics**: How to measure effectiveness

#### 6. Compliance & Standards (1 minute)
- IEC 62443 Security Level achievement
- IMO MSC-FAL.1/Circ.3 compliance
- BIMCO guidelines adherence
- Industry best practices

#### 7. Conclusion & Recommendations (1 minute)
- **Summary**: Key accomplishments of your architecture
- **Impact**: How M/V CyberSea is more secure
- **Next steps**: Post-implementation activities (monitoring, continuous improvement)
- **Lessons learned**: What you learned during the project

#### 8. Q&A Introduction (30 seconds)
- Thank stakeholders for their time
- Invite questions

### Presentation Delivery Guidelines

**Timing**:
- **Strict 20-minute limit**: Presentations will be stopped at 20 minutes
- **Practice timing**: Rehearse multiple times to stay within limit
- **Use timer**: Designate a timekeeper on your team

**Team Participation**:
- **All members must present**: Divide content equitably
- **Smooth transitions**: Practice handoffs between speakers
- **Engagement**: Speak clearly, make eye contact, project confidence

**Visual Design**:
- **Professional slides**: Clean design, consistent fonts and colors
- **Minimal text**: Bullet points, not paragraphs; use visuals
- **High-quality diagrams**: Readable from back of room
- **Accessible color schemes**: Avoid red/green combinations (colorblind accessibility)

**Technical Accuracy**:
- **Know your content**: Be prepared to explain any detail on slides
- **Cite sources**: Reference standards, papers, tools where appropriate
- **No jargon**: Explain acronyms and technical terms for non-technical stakeholders

**Stakeholder Focus**:
- **Speak to your audience**: Balance technical depth with accessibility
- **Address concerns**: Anticipate stakeholder priorities (cost, safety, compliance)
- **Business value**: Emphasize risk reduction and ROI, not just technical features

### Demonstration Requirements (10 minutes)

Following your presentation, you will conduct a 10-minute live demonstration of key security controls or concepts. The demo should be:

- **Relevant**: Showcase critical components of your architecture
- **Functional**: Actually works (not just slides or videos)
- **Engaging**: Visually interesting and easy to follow
- **Prepared**: Rehearsed and timed; backup plan if tech fails

#### Demonstration Options

Choose 2-3 of the following to demonstrate:

**Option 1: Network Segmentation in Action**
- Show simulated network with multiple zones
- Demonstrate firewall blocking traffic between zones
- Attempt to access OT system from IT zone (blocked)
- Show allowed traffic flow (e.g., logging to SIEM)
- **Tools**: GNS3, Packet Tracer, or virtual lab

**Option 2: Intrusion Detection Alert**
- Generate simulated attack traffic (e.g., port scan, malicious packet)
- Show IDS detecting and alerting
- Display alert in SIEM dashboard
- Walk through alert triage process
- **Tools**: Snort, Suricata, Security Onion, ELK stack

**Option 3: Incident Response Playbook Walkthrough**
- Present a simulated incident (e.g., ransomware alert)
- Walk through response playbook step-by-step
- Show decision tree or workflow diagram
- Demonstrate communication (e.g., mock radio call, alert notification)
- **Tools**: Slides, physical props, flowchart visualization

**Option 4: SIEM Dashboard & Correlation**
- Show live or simulated SIEM dashboard with vessel security data
- Highlight key metrics (alert counts, top threats, system health)
- Demonstrate correlation rule triggering (e.g., failed logins → critical alert)
- Explain how analysts use dashboard for monitoring
- **Tools**: Splunk, ELK, Grafana, custom dashboard

**Option 5: CAN Bus or NMEA Protocol Analysis**
- Capture CAN bus or NMEA 2000 traffic (from lab or simulation)
- Show protocol dissection in Wireshark or similar tool
- Identify normal vs. anomalous message (e.g., spoofed message)
- Explain how IDS would detect the anomaly
- **Tools**: Wireshark, CANalyze, custom Python scripts

**Option 6: Application Whitelisting Demo**
- Show OT workstation (ECDIS simulator or generic Windows system)
- Attempt to run unauthorized application (blocked by whitelisting)
- Attempt to run authorized application (succeeds)
- Explain how this prevents malware execution
- **Tools**: Windows AppLocker, virtual machine

**Option 7: GPS Spoofing Detection**
- Simulate GPS position data (normal and spoofed)
- Show correlation with AIS and other sensors
- Trigger alert when position discrepancy detected
- Display alert on bridge dashboard
- **Tools**: GPS simulation tool, custom script, visualization

#### Demonstration Best Practices

- **Test thoroughly**: Run demo multiple times before presentation day
- **Prepare backups**: Have screenshots or video recording as fallback
- **Narrate clearly**: Explain what you're doing as you do it
- **Show cause and effect**: Demonstrate both the problem and your solution
- **Keep it simple**: Don't try to show too much; focus on impact
- **Time it**: 10 minutes includes setup and explanation

### Q&A Session (10 minutes)

After your demo, the stakeholder panel will ask questions.

#### Stakeholder Panel Composition

The panel may include:
- **Instructor**: Technical questions, design justifications
- **Industry guest**: Practical feasibility, operational questions
- **Faculty member**: Standards compliance, academic rigor
- **Student peers**: Clarifying questions

#### Types of Questions to Expect

**Technical Questions**:
- "Why did you choose this IDS technology over alternatives?"
- "How would your architecture handle a zero-day exploit?"
- "What happens if the firewall fails? Is there redundancy?"

**Operational Questions**:
- "How will crew with limited IT skills manage this system?"
- "What if an incident occurs while the vessel is in the middle of the ocean?"
- "How do you ensure security doesn't interfere with emergency operations?"

**Cost & ROI Questions**:
- "Can you justify the $500K investment?"
- "What are the ongoing annual costs for maintenance?"
- "How do you measure the success of this architecture?"

**Compliance Questions**:
- "Does this meet IEC 62443 Security Level 3 requirements?"
- "How do you address IMO's recommendation for regular drills?"
- "What documentation is required for flag state inspection?"

**Implementation Questions**:
- "Why did you phase the implementation this way?"
- "What are the biggest risks during implementation?"
- "How do you handle vendor dependencies?"

#### Q&A Best Practices

- **Listen carefully**: Make sure you understand the question before answering
- **Clarify if needed**: "Just to make sure I understand, you're asking about..."
- **Be honest**: If you don't know, say so ("That's a great question. We didn't explore that, but here's how I would approach it...")
- **Stay calm**: Don't be defensive; view questions as opportunities to showcase knowledge
- **Divide & conquer**: Team members can support each other (but avoid talking over each other)
- **Reference your work**: Point to specific sections of your report ("We addressed that in Phase 2, Section 5...")
- **Time awareness**: Keep answers concise (1-2 minutes max per question)

## Tips for Success

### Presentation Preparation

1. **Start Early**: Begin outlining presentation in Week 11, not Week 13
2. **Tell a Story**: Create narrative arc—problem → investigation → solution → impact
3. **Know Your Audience**: Balance technical depth with accessibility
4. **Rehearse, Rehearse, Rehearse**: Practice at least 5 times as a team
5. **Time Yourself**: Use a timer during practice; adjust content to fit
6. **Anticipate Questions**: Do a "murder board" exercise (team asks each other tough questions)
7. **Backup Plans**: Prepare for tech failures (PDF backup, screenshots, video recording of demo)

### Demonstration Preparation

1. **Test in Target Environment**: Test on presentation laptop and projector if possible
2. **Simplify**: Focus on impact, not complexity
3. **Script It**: Write out exactly what you'll say and do
4. **Have Fallback**: Screenshots or video in case live demo fails
5. **Timing**: Leave buffer time (demo might take longer under pressure)

### Q&A Preparation

1. **Brainstorm Questions**: List 20-30 possible questions and prepare answers
2. **Review Your Report**: Be intimately familiar with every section
3. **Practice Q&A**: Have peers or friends grill you with questions
4. **Stay Calm**: Pause, think, then answer; it's okay to take a moment

### Team Dynamics

1. **Equal Participation**: Ensure all members present roughly equal amounts
2. **Support Each Other**: Help teammates if they get stuck during Q&A
3. **Practice Handoffs**: Smooth transitions between speakers
4. **Designate Roles**: Timekeeper, demo operator, Q&A coordinator
5. **Positive Energy**: Encourage each other; project confidence

### Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Too Much Content**: Trying to cover everything; focus on highlights
- **Reading Slides**: Slides are visual aids, not your script
- **Technical Jargon Overload**: Explain acronyms, use plain language
- **Running Over Time**: Strictly adhere to 20-minute limit
- **Untested Demo**: Demo fails because you didn't rehearse
- **Arguing with Panel**: Defensive responses to questions
- **Ignoring Feedback**: Not incorporating Phase 1-3 feedback into final report
