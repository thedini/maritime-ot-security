# Maritime Cybersecurity Compliance Checklist

## Overview

This checklist provides a practical, actionable guide for ensuring cybersecurity compliance and operational security aboard vessels. Use this for:
- Pre-voyage cybersecurity verification
- Periodic self-assessment
- Audit preparation
- SMS integration verification
- Continuous improvement tracking

## How to Use This Checklist

**Status Indicators:**
- [ ] Not Started / Not Applicable
- [P] Partially Implemented
- [X] Fully Implemented
- [N/A] Not Applicable

**Priority Levels:**
- **[CRITICAL]** - Essential for safety and regulatory compliance
- **[HIGH]** - Important for security and best practice
- **[MEDIUM]** - Recommended for enhanced security
- **[LOW]** - Additional security enhancement

**Review Frequency:**
- **[DAILY]** - Check daily (during handovers)
- **[WEEKLY]** - Check weekly
- **[MONTHLY]** - Check monthly
- **[QUARTERLY]** - Check every 3 months
- **[ANNUALLY]** - Check at least annually
- **[AS NEEDED]** - Check when relevant

## Section 1: Regulatory Compliance

### 1.1 IMO Resolution MSC.428(98) Compliance

**[CRITICAL] [ANNUALLY]**

- [ ] Cyber risk management is integrated into the Safety Management System (SMS)
- [ ] Cyber risk assessment has been conducted and documented
- [ ] SMS includes cybersecurity policies and procedures
- [ ] Cyber risk management was in place by first DOC annual verification after January 1, 2021
- [ ] Designated Person Ashore (DPA) responsibilities include cyber incident oversight
- [ ] Master and senior officers are aware of cyber risk management requirements

**Evidence Required:**
- SMS manual with cyber risk management sections
- Cyber risk assessment document
- Training records
- DOC/SMC certificates and audit reports

### 1.2 Flag State Requirements

**[CRITICAL] [ANNUALLY]**

- [ ] Flag state specific cyber requirements identified and documented
- [ ] All flag state cyber security circulars and guidance reviewed
- [ ] Flag state incident reporting procedures understood and accessible
- [ ] Contact information for flag state cyber incident reporting current

**Evidence Required:**
- Register of applicable flag state requirements
- Flag state guidance documents
- Incident reporting contact list

### 1.3 Port State Control Preparedness

**[HIGH] [QUARTERLY]**

- [ ] Master can demonstrate cyber risk management in SMS during PSC inspection
- [ ] Cyber risk assessment is readily available for inspection
- [ ] Training records include cybersecurity elements
- [ ] Evidence of cyber procedures implementation is documented
- [ ] Crew can describe basic cybersecurity measures

**Evidence Required:**
- SMS manual accessible
- Recent training records
- Incident logs (even if no incidents)
- Audit reports

### 1.4 Class Society Compliance

**[HIGH] [ANNUALLY]**

- [ ] Class society cyber notation requirements identified (if applicable)
- [ ] Class society cyber rules compliance verified
- [ ] Class surveys include cyber elements review
- [ ] Class society cyber security guidance reviewed and implemented

**Evidence Required:**
- Class certificates and survey reports
- Cyber notation documentation (if applicable)
- Class cyber guidance documents

### 1.5 Insurance Requirements

**[HIGH] [ANNUALLY]**

- [ ] P&I Club cyber security requirements identified
- [ ] Hull & Machinery policy cyber clauses reviewed
- [ ] Insurance cyber incident reporting procedures understood
- [ ] Evidence of cyber risk management provided to insurers as required
- [ ] Cyber insurance coverage (if applicable) terms understood

**Evidence Required:**
- Insurance policies with cyber clauses
- P&I Club requirements document
- Evidence submissions to insurers

## Section 2: Documentation and SMS Integration

### 2.1 Policy and Governance

**[CRITICAL] [ANNUALLY]**

- [ ] Company cybersecurity policy is documented and accessible
- [ ] Shipboard cybersecurity policy statement is posted
- [ ] Cybersecurity roles and responsibilities are clearly defined
- [ ] Cyber Security Officer (or equivalent) is designated
- [ ] Cybersecurity is included in management review meetings

**Evidence Required:**
- Company and shipboard cyber policy documents
- Organization chart with cyber responsibilities
- Management review minutes

### 2.2 Risk Assessment

**[CRITICAL] [ANNUALLY]**

- [ ] Cyber risk assessment completed for vessel
- [ ] Risk assessment covers all critical systems
- [ ] Threats and vulnerabilities identified and documented
- [ ] Risks are rated (likelihood and impact)
- [ ] Risk treatment decisions documented (mitigate, accept, transfer, avoid)
- [ ] Risk assessment reviewed and updated at least annually
- [ ] Risk assessment updated after major system changes or incidents

**Evidence Required:**
- Cyber risk assessment report
- Asset inventory
- Risk register
- Risk treatment plan

### 2.3 Procedures and Instructions

**[HIGH] [ANNUALLY]**

- [ ] Cybersecurity procedures integrated into SMS (not separate manual)
- [ ] Procedures cover: access control, password management, network security
- [ ] Procedures cover: removable media (USB drives), email/internet use
- [ ] Procedures cover: patch management, backup/recovery
- [ ] Procedures cover: incident detection, reporting, response
- [ ] Procedures cover: physical security of systems
- [ ] Procedures are practical and understandable by crew
- [ ] Procedures reviewed and updated at least annually

**Evidence Required:**
- SMS manual sections covering cyber procedures
- Checklists and job aids for crew
- Procedure review records

### 2.4 Asset Inventory

**[HIGH] [QUARTERLY]**

- [ ] Inventory of all IT and OT systems is current
- [ ] Inventory includes: system name, location, function, criticality
- [ ] Inventory includes: manufacturer, model, software version
- [ ] Inventory includes: network connectivity, interfaces
- [ ] Network architecture diagram is current
- [ ] Inventory updated when systems added, changed, or removed

**Evidence Required:**
- Asset inventory spreadsheet or database
- Network architecture diagram
- System labels or tags

### 2.5 Records and Logs

**[MEDIUM] [MONTHLY]**

- [ ] Cyber incident log is maintained (even if no incidents)
- [ ] System maintenance and change records include cyber elements
- [ ] Access logs (where available) are reviewed periodically
- [ ] Training records include cybersecurity training
- [ ] Audit and inspection records include cyber elements
- [ ] Records retention meets regulatory requirements

**Evidence Required:**
- Cyber incident log
- Maintenance records
- Training records
- Audit reports

## Section 3: Technical Controls

### 3.1 Asset Inventory and Configuration

**[HIGH] [QUARTERLY]**

- [ ] All shipboard systems are inventoried (see Section 2.4)
- [ ] Critical systems are identified and labeled
- [ ] Baseline configurations are documented for all critical systems
- [ ] Configuration changes are managed through change control process
- [ ] System documentation (manuals, diagrams) is current and accessible

**Evidence Required:**
- Asset inventory
- Configuration baselines
- System documentation

### 3.2 Access Control

**[CRITICAL] [MONTHLY]**

- [ ] ALL default passwords have been changed on all systems
- [ ] Strong password policy is implemented and enforced
- [ ] Individual user accounts are used (no shared generic accounts)
- [ ] User accounts are based on role (Master, Officer, Engineer, etc.)
- [ ] Accounts are disabled or deleted when crew sign off
- [ ] Privileged accounts (administrator) are limited and controlled
- [ ] Physical access to critical systems is restricted (locked doors, escorts)
- [ ] User account list is reviewed monthly for obsolete accounts

**Password Policy Requirements:**
- Minimum 8 characters (12+ preferred)
- Mix of uppercase, lowercase, numbers, special characters
- No dictionary words or personal information
- Changed after crew changes (for privileged accounts)
- Not written on equipment or posted visibly

**Evidence Required:**
- Password policy document
- User account list with creation/deletion dates
- Physical access control logs

### 3.3 Network Security and Segmentation

**[CRITICAL] [QUARTERLY]**

- [ ] Network architecture includes segmentation between critical and non-critical systems
- [ ] Critical navigation systems are isolated from crew internet access
- [ ] Firewalls are in place between network segments
- [ ] Firewall rules follow default-deny principle (block all, allow specific)
- [ ] Crew internet/email is on separate network from operational systems
- [ ] Guest WiFi (if applicable) is isolated from crew and operational networks
- [ ] Network segmentation is documented in diagram
- [ ] Firewall configuration is reviewed quarterly

**Segmentation Best Practice:**
```
[Zone 1: Critical Navigation] <-Firewall-> [Zone 2: Machinery/Cargo]
           |                                        |
    Isolated or limited                      Shore monitoring
           |                                        |
[Zone 3: Admin/Operations] <-Firewall-> [Zone 4: Crew Internet]
```

**Evidence Required:**
- Network architecture diagram
- Firewall configuration documentation
- Network test results

### 3.4 Malware Protection

**[HIGH] [WEEKLY]**

- [ ] Antivirus/antimalware software is installed on all systems where supported
- [ ] Antivirus definitions are updated regularly (weekly minimum)
- [ ] Antivirus scans are scheduled and run automatically
- [ ] Quarantine and detection logs are reviewed weekly
- [ ] Antivirus is active and not disabled
- [ ] All USB drives and removable media are scanned before use

**Evidence Required:**
- Antivirus software inventory
- Update logs
- Scan logs and quarantine reports

### 3.5 Patch Management

**[HIGH] [MONTHLY]**

- [ ] Patch management procedure is documented
- [ ] Security patches and updates are monitored (OEM websites, notifications)
- [ ] Patches are tested before deployment (where feasible)
- [ ] Patches are applied in timely manner (critical patches within 30 days)
- [ ] Patch status is tracked for all systems
- [ ] Systems that cannot be patched (legacy) have compensating controls
- [ ] Patch application is documented (what, when, by whom)

**Evidence Required:**
- Patch management procedure
- Patch status log
- Patch application records

### 3.6 Backup and Recovery

**[CRITICAL] [WEEKLY]**

- [ ] Backup procedures are documented
- [ ] Critical data is backed up regularly (weekly minimum)
- [ ] Backups include: navigation routes, system configurations, logs, critical data
- [ ] Backups are stored separately from primary systems
- [ ] Backup integrity is verified (test restoration quarterly)
- [ ] Recovery procedures are documented and tested
- [ ] Recovery time objectives (RTO) are defined for critical systems

**Critical Data to Back Up:**
- ECDIS routes and waypoints
- System configurations
- Voyage data recorder (VDR) data (per regulations)
- Logs and records
- SMS documentation
- Contact lists

**Evidence Required:**
- Backup procedure
- Backup schedule and logs
- Restoration test results

### 3.7 Remote Access Control

**[CRITICAL] [AS NEEDED]**

- [ ] Remote access is controlled and requires authorization
- [ ] Remote access uses encrypted connections (VPN, SSH, HTTPS)
- [ ] Remote access requires authentication (username/password minimum)
- [ ] Remote access sessions are logged
- [ ] Remote access is supervised (engineer present during OEM remote support)
- [ ] Remote access is disabled when not actively needed
- [ ] One-time or time-limited credentials are used for contractor remote access

**Evidence Required:**
- Remote access procedure
- Remote access logs
- Authorization records

### 3.8 Physical Security

**[HIGH] [DAILY]**

- [ ] Bridge access is controlled (locked when unattended)
- [ ] Engine control room access is restricted
- [ ] Server room / IT equipment room is locked
- [ ] Visitors and contractors are escorted at all times
- [ ] CCTV monitors critical system areas
- [ ] Tampering would be evident (tamper seals on critical equipment)
- [ ] Unauthorized devices (USB drives, laptops) are detected and removed

**Evidence Required:**
- ISPS Code access control integration
- Visitor logs
- CCTV footage (as needed)

### 3.9 System Hardening

**[MEDIUM] [ANNUALLY]**

- [ ] Unnecessary services and protocols are disabled (Telnet, FTP, unused ports)
- [ ] Secure protocols are used where possible (SSH vs Telnet, HTTPS vs HTTP)
- [ ] USB ports are disabled or restricted on critical systems
- [ ] Autorun from removable media is disabled
- [ ] Wireless (WiFi, Bluetooth) is disabled on operational systems if not needed
- [ ] Unused software is removed
- [ ] Systems follow principle of least functionality

**Evidence Required:**
- System configuration documentation
- System hardening checklist

### 3.10 Logging and Monitoring

**[MEDIUM] [WEEKLY]**

- [ ] Logging is enabled on systems that support it
- [ ] Logs are reviewed weekly for anomalies
- [ ] Logs include: access attempts, configuration changes, errors, security events
- [ ] Logs are protected from tampering
- [ ] Logs are retained per regulatory requirements
- [ ] Alerts are configured for critical events (where possible)

**Evidence Required:**
- Logging configuration
- Log review records
- Alert configuration

## Section 4: Operational Security

### 4.1 Pre-Voyage Cybersecurity Check

**[CRITICAL] [DAILY/PER VOYAGE]**

**Navigation Systems:**
- [ ] ECDIS operating normally, no errors or unusual behavior
- [ ] GPS position validated (cross-check with radar, visual, dead reckoning)
- [ ] AIS displaying correct ship information (name, MMSI, position, course, speed)
- [ ] Radar operating normally, contacts correlate with AIS
- [ ] Autopilot responding correctly
- [ ] Compass readings consistent (gyro and magnetic within expected deviation)
- [ ] Charts and updates are current

**Machinery Systems:**
- [ ] Engine control systems operating normally
- [ ] No unusual alarms or error messages
- [ ] Remote monitoring connections are authorized and secure
- [ ] Fuel and ballast systems operating correctly

**Communications Systems:**
- [ ] VHF, GMDSS, satellite communications operational
- [ ] Email and internet connectivity functioning (if applicable)
- [ ] No unauthorized devices on network

**General:**
- [ ] No suspicious activity or anomalies observed
- [ ] All systems within normal operating parameters
- [ ] Officer of the Watch and Chief Engineer briefed on cyber status

**Evidence Required:**
- Pre-voyage checklist (can integrate into existing bridge checklist)
- Log entry noting cyber check completed

### 4.2 Underway Monitoring

**[CRITICAL] [DAILY]**

**Continuous (OOW Duties):**
- [ ] Monitor GPS accuracy and cross-check position regularly
- [ ] Verify AIS display matches actual ship status
- [ ] Watch for unusual system behavior (alarms, performance, response)
- [ ] Maintain situational awareness (visual, radar, AIS correlation)

**Daily (During Handovers):**
- [ ] Review system status and performance during watch handover
- [ ] Report any anomalies to Master and in log
- [ ] Check for software update notifications or alerts

**Evidence Required:**
- Log entries noting system status
- Handover checklists

### 4.3 Port Operations

**[HIGH] [AS NEEDED]**

**Pre-Arrival:**
- [ ] Review port cybersecurity requirements or advisories
- [ ] Disable or restrict shore access to ship systems (if applicable)
- [ ] Brief crew on heightened security during port stay

**In Port:**
- [ ] Control access to vessel (ISPS Code compliance)
- [ ] Escort all visitors, contractors, port workers
- [ ] Supervise any contractor work on ship systems
- [ ] Monitor for unauthorized devices (laptops, USB drives, wireless access points)
- [ ] Be cautious of port WiFi (use VPN, avoid sensitive data)
- [ ] Limit or secure remote access from ship to shore

**Pre-Departure:**
- [ ] Verify no unauthorized devices left aboard
- [ ] Check for any system anomalies after port stay
- [ ] Review and restore any security settings changed during port operations

**Evidence Required:**
- Visitor logs
- Contractor work permits and supervision records

### 4.4 Removable Media Control

**[HIGH] [EVERY USE]**

- [ ] Only authorized USB drives are used on ship systems
- [ ] All USB drives are scanned for malware before use
- [ ] Personal USB drives are not used on operational systems
- [ ] Chart update USB drives are dedicated (not used for other purposes)
- [ ] Software update media is from trusted sources only (OEM)
- [ ] CDs/DVDs for system updates are from trusted sources
- [ ] Removable media is logged (what, when, which system)

**Evidence Required:**
- Removable media log
- Antivirus scan records

### 4.5 Email and Internet Use

**[HIGH] [DAILY]**

- [ ] Crew trained on phishing and email security
- [ ] Suspicious emails are reported and not opened
- [ ] Attachments from unknown senders are not opened
- [ ] Links in emails are verified before clicking
- [ ] Personal email is kept separate from ship operations email
- [ ] Sensitive information is not sent via unencrypted email
- [ ] Crew internet use follows acceptable use policy
- [ ] Downloads are restricted to authorized software from trusted sources

**Evidence Required:**
- Crew training records
- Acceptable use policy
- Reported phishing attempts log

### 4.6 Software and System Updates

**[CRITICAL] [AS NEEDED]**

- [ ] Chart updates are from official sources (UKHO, NOAA, PRIMAR, etc.)
- [ ] Software updates are from official OEM sources only
- [ ] Updates are verified (checksum, digital signature) before installation
- [ ] Updates are tested on non-critical system first (if possible)
- [ ] Updates are supervised by qualified personnel
- [ ] System backup is created before major updates
- [ ] Update source is authenticated (not from unknown email, untrusted website)

**Evidence Required:**
- Update logs
- Source verification records

### 4.7 Third-Party Access Management

**[HIGH] [AS NEEDED]**

- [ ] Third-party access (OEM support, remote monitoring) is authorized by Master or Chief Engineer
- [ ] Third-party access is scheduled (no unannounced access)
- [ ] Third-party access uses secure methods (VPN, encrypted)
- [ ] Third-party access is supervised by ship's personnel
- [ ] Third-party is required to follow ship security procedures
- [ ] Contractor devices (laptops) are scanned for malware before connecting
- [ ] Third-party access credentials are one-time or time-limited
- [ ] All third-party actions are logged and reviewed
- [ ] Third-party access is terminated immediately after work complete

**Evidence Required:**
- Third-party access authorization form
- Third-party access logs
- Work completion reports

### 4.8 Incident Detection and Reporting

**[CRITICAL] [CONTINUOUS]**

**Crew Responsibilities:**
- [ ] All crew know how to report suspected cyber incidents
- [ ] Crew report any suspicious or unusual system behavior immediately
- [ ] Reporting process is simple and clear (report to OOW or duty engineer)
- [ ] Crew are encouraged to report without fear of blame

**Watch Officer Responsibilities:**
- [ ] Watch officers monitor for anomalies during watch
- [ ] Watch officers report all potential incidents to Master
- [ ] Watch officers document observations in log

**Master Responsibilities:**
- [ ] Master assesses all reported potential incidents
- [ ] Master activates incident response for confirmed incidents
- [ ] Master reports incidents per company and regulatory requirements

**Evidence Required:**
- Incident reporting procedure
- Training records
- Incident reports (even false alarms)

## Section 5: Training and Awareness

### 5.1 Initial Training

**[CRITICAL] [UPON JOINING]**

- [ ] All crew receive cybersecurity awareness training during familiarization
- [ ] Training covers: why cybersecurity matters, common threats, how to report
- [ ] Training covers: ship-specific procedures (passwords, access control, USB policy)
- [ ] Training is documented (who, when, topics covered)
- [ ] Training materials are practical and relevant to maritime environment

**Training Topics:**
- Cyber threats to maritime (GPS spoofing, ransomware, etc.)
- How cyber incidents affect safety
- Common attack methods (phishing, malware, USB, social engineering)
- Good cyber hygiene practices
- Ship-specific procedures
- How to recognize anomalies
- How to report incidents

**Evidence Required:**
- Training curriculum
- Training attendance records
- Training materials

### 5.2 Refresher Training

**[HIGH] [ANNUALLY]**

- [ ] All crew receive annual cybersecurity refresher training
- [ ] Refresher includes updates on new threats
- [ ] Refresher includes lessons learned from incidents (own or industry)
- [ ] Refresher reinforces good practices
- [ ] Training is documented

**Evidence Required:**
- Training schedule
- Training attendance records

### 5.3 Role-Specific Training

**[HIGH] [AS NEEDED]**

**Master and Senior Officers:**
- [ ] Training on cyber incident response and decision-making
- [ ] Training on regulatory requirements
- [ ] Training on managing cyber risk

**Chief Engineer and Engineers:**
- [ ] Training on securing machinery and control systems
- [ ] Training on patch management and system updates
- [ ] Training on managing remote access

**Radio Officer / IT Officer (if carried):**
- [ ] Technical training on network security
- [ ] Training on user account management
- [ ] Training on system monitoring and incident detection

**Evidence Required:**
- Role-specific training curriculum
- Training records by role

### 5.4 Drills and Exercises

**[HIGH] [QUARTERLY]**

- [ ] Cyber incident scenarios are included in emergency drills
- [ ] Tabletop exercises test incident response plan
- [ ] Drills include detection, reporting, response, communication
- [ ] Drills are realistic and relevant to vessel operations
- [ ] Drills are debriefed and lessons learned documented
- [ ] Drill scenarios vary (ransomware, GPS spoofing, system failure, etc.)

**Drill Scenarios:**
- ECDIS ransomware requiring switch to backup
- GPS spoofing detected, use alternative navigation
- Malware detected on crew computer, containment
- Unauthorized remote access attempt
- AIS displaying incorrect information

**Evidence Required:**
- Drill schedule
- Drill scenarios
- Drill participation records
- Debrief notes and lessons learned

### 5.5 Awareness Materials

**[MEDIUM] [ONGOING]**

- [ ] Cybersecurity posters displayed in crew areas
- [ ] Tips and reminders in safety meetings
- [ ] Cyber incidents (industry) shared with crew as lessons learned
- [ ] Cybersecurity included in safety culture

**Evidence Required:**
- Posters and awareness materials
- Safety meeting minutes

## Section 6: Incident Response and Recovery

### 6.1 Incident Response Plan

**[CRITICAL] [ANNUALLY]**

- [ ] Cyber incident response plan is documented
- [ ] Plan defines roles and responsibilities
- [ ] Plan includes incident classification (minor, moderate, major, critical)
- [ ] Plan includes response procedures (detect, assess, contain, eradicate, recover)
- [ ] Plan includes communication procedures (who to notify, when, how)
- [ ] Plan includes escalation procedures
- [ ] Plan is tested at least annually (tabletop or drill)
- [ ] Plan is reviewed and updated annually or after incidents

**Evidence Required:**
- Incident response plan document
- Test/drill records
- Plan review and update records

### 6.2 Emergency Contacts

**[CRITICAL] [QUARTERLY]**

- [ ] Emergency contact list is current and accessible
- [ ] List includes: Company DPA, IT support, OEM contacts, flag state, coast guard
- [ ] List includes: Cybersecurity consultants, forensic experts (if applicable)
- [ ] List includes: P&I Club, H&M insurers
- [ ] Contact information verified quarterly (phone numbers, emails current)
- [ ] Contacts are accessible to Master and senior officers 24/7

**Evidence Required:**
- Emergency contact list
- Contact verification records

### 6.3 Incident Documentation

**[CRITICAL] [AS NEEDED]**

- [ ] Template for cyber incident report is prepared
- [ ] Incident documentation includes: timeline, systems affected, actions taken, decisions made
- [ ] Evidence is preserved (logs, screenshots)
- [ ] Communications during incident are documented
- [ ] Post-incident report is prepared (root cause, lessons learned, corrective actions)

**Evidence Required:**
- Incident report template
- Completed incident reports (if any incidents occurred)

### 6.4 Recovery Procedures

**[CRITICAL] [QUARTERLY]**

- [ ] Recovery procedures are documented for all critical systems
- [ ] Recovery priorities are defined (safety-critical first)
- [ ] Backup restoration procedures are tested quarterly
- [ ] System rebuild procedures are documented
- [ ] Alternative/manual operation procedures are documented and tested
- [ ] Recovery resources are available (backup media, spare equipment, contact info)

**Evidence Required:**
- Recovery procedures
- Backup restoration test results
- Manual operation drills

### 6.5 Lessons Learned and Improvement

**[HIGH] [AFTER INCIDENTS]**

- [ ] Post-incident review is conducted for all incidents
- [ ] Lessons learned are documented
- [ ] Corrective actions are identified and implemented
- [ ] Procedures are updated based on lessons learned
- [ ] Lessons learned are shared with company fleet (as appropriate)
- [ ] Lessons learned are shared with industry (anonymized, via ISACs)

**Evidence Required:**
- Post-incident review reports
- Corrective action plans
- Procedure update records

- [ ] Master and crew can demonstrate awareness and implementation

- [ ] Master prepared to demonstrate cyber risk management if asked

- [ ] Training and drill records current

**Class Society Surveys:**
- [ ] Class requirements for cyber (if applicable) are met

- [ ] Cyber notation requirements (if applicable) maintained

**Evidence Required:**

- Certificates (DOC, SMC, class certificates)

### 7.3 Management Review

- [ ] Cybersecurity is included in company management review
- [ ] Management review covers: risk assessment results, incidents, audit findings, training
- [ ] Management review covers: effectiveness of controls, resources needed
- [ ] Management review decisions and actions are documented
- [ ] Management demonstrates commitment to cybersecurity

**Evidence Required:**
- Management review meeting minutes

### 7.4 Compliance Verification

**[HIGH] [ANNUALLY]**

- [ ] Self-assessment against this checklist completed annually
- [ ] Compliance gaps identified and action plan created

- [ ] Compliance status reported to company management

**Evidence Required:**
- Completed checklist with status

- Progress reports

## Section 9: Special Scenarios

### 9.1 GPS/GNSS Spoofing or Jamming

**[CRITICAL] [AS NEEDED]**

**Recognition:**
- [ ] Crew trained to recognize GPS anomalies (position jumps, inconsistent with DR)
- [ ] GPS position routinely cross-checked with other methods
- [ ] RAIM warnings understood and heeded
- [ ] Multiple positioning methods used (radar, visual, dead reckoning)

**Response:**
- [ ] GPS anomaly reported to Master immediately
- [ ] Switch to alternative positioning methods
- [ ] Report to flag state and coast guard if in area of widespread spoofing
- [ ] Document incident

**Evidence Required:**
- GPS anomaly detection training
- Alternative navigation procedures
- Incident reports

### 9.2 Ransomware or Malware Infection

**[CRITICAL] [AS NEEDED]**

**Recognition:**
- [ ] Crew trained to recognize ransomware symptoms (locked files, ransom note, slow system)
- [ ] Ransomware detected quickly through monitoring or crew reporting

**Response:**
- [ ] Infected system isolated immediately (network disconnect, power off)
- [ ] Incident response plan activated
- [ ] Malware removal or system restoration from backup
- [ ] Investigation of infection source (email, USB, network)
- [ ] Corrective actions to prevent reoccurrence

**Critical Decision:**
- [ ] Master prepared to make decision on ransom payment (strong recommendation: DO NOT PAY)
- [ ] Company policy on ransomware known to Master

**Evidence Required:**
- Ransomware response procedure
- Training records
- Decision-making authority

### 9.3 Unauthorized Remote Access

**[CRITICAL] [AS NEEDED]**

**Recognition:**
- [ ] Remote access is logged and monitored
- [ ] Unauthorized access attempts are detected

**Response:**
- [ ] Unauthorized access blocked immediately
- [ ] Remote access disabled until investigated
- [ ] Passwords changed
- [ ] Systems checked for compromise
- [ ] Incident reported to company and authorities

**Evidence Required:**
- Remote access logs
- Incident response procedure

### 9.4 Insider Threat / Disgruntled Crew

**[HIGH] [AS NEEDED]**

**Prevention:**
- [ ] Access control limits damage potential
- [ ] Privileged accounts are monitored
- [ ] Crew welfare and morale are maintained (reduces motivation)
- [ ] Reporting culture encourages reporting of concerning behavior

**Detection:**
- [ ] Unusual access patterns detected
- [ ] Unauthorized actions identified

**Response:**
- [ ] Potential insider threat reported to Master and company
- [ ] Individual's access restricted if threat confirmed
- [ ] Investigation conducted
- [ ] Systems checked for sabotage or data theft

**Evidence Required:**
- Access control and monitoring procedures
- Incident response for insider threats

### 9.5 Supply Chain Compromise

**[MEDIUM] [AS NEEDED]**

**Prevention:**
- [ ] Software and firmware updates from official OEM sources only
- [ ] Update authenticity verified (checksum, digital signature)
- [ ] Spare parts and equipment from authorized suppliers
- [ ] New equipment inspected for tampering

**Detection:**
- [ ] Unexpected system behavior after updates
- [ ] Malware detection after equipment installation

**Response:**
- [ ] Compromised update removed
- [ ] System restored to known-good state
- [ ] OEM notified
- [ ] Industry notified (via ISAC or flag state)

**Evidence Required:**
- Supply chain security procedures
- Update verification procedures

## Section 10: Audit Preparation Guide

### Pre-Audit Preparation

**2 Weeks Before Audit:**
- [ ] Review this checklist and verify status of all items
- [ ] Identify and address any gaps
- [ ] Organize all documentation and evidence
- [ ] Prepare SMS manual with cyber sections flagged
- [ ] Update asset inventory and network diagrams
- [ ] Review recent incident logs, training records, audit findings
- [ ] Brief Master and senior officers on audit expectations
- [ ] Prepare summary of cyber risk management program for auditor

**1 Week Before Audit:**
- [ ] Conduct mock audit or self-assessment
- [ ] Verify all documents are current and accessible
- [ ] Ensure Master and crew are prepared to discuss cybersecurity
- [ ] Prepare examples of implementation (how procedures are followed in practice)
- [ ] Check that training records are up to date

**Day of Audit:**
- [ ] Have SMS manual ready with cyber sections bookmarked
- [ ] Have key documents organized (risk assessment, procedures, training records, audit reports)
- [ ] Have network diagram and asset inventory accessible
- [ ] Be prepared to demonstrate systems and controls (show firewall, segmentation, access control)
- [ ] Be prepared to walk through incident response plan
- [ ] Be honest about gaps and show plan for improvement

### Key Documents for Auditor

- [ ] SMS manual with cyber risk management sections
- [ ] Cyber risk assessment report
- [ ] Asset inventory and network architecture diagram
- [ ] Cybersecurity policies and procedures
- [ ] Training records (initial and refresher)
- [ ] Drill and exercise records
- [ ] Incident response plan
- [ ] Incident log (even if no incidents)
- [ ] Internal audit reports with cyber elements
- [ ] Corrective action tracking
- [ ] Management review minutes with cyber topics

### Common Auditor Questions

**Be prepared to answer:**
- "Show me your cyber risk assessment."
- "How is cyber risk management integrated into your SMS?"
- "Who is responsible for cybersecurity on this vessel?"
- "How do you train crew on cybersecurity?"
- "Walk me through your incident response procedure."
- "Show me how your network is segmented."
- "How do you manage passwords and access control?"
- "How do you handle software updates and patches?"
- "What would you do if ECDIS was infected with ransomware?"
- "How do you control remote access?"

**For Crew:**
- "What cyber threats are you aware of?"
- "How would you recognize a cyber incident?"
- "How do you report a suspicious email?"
- "What is the policy on USB drives?"

## Checklist Summary

### Critical Must-Haves (Regulatory Compliance)

1. **Cyber risk assessment** documented and current
2. **SMS integration** - cyber procedures in SMS manual
3. **Training** - all crew trained, records current
4. **Incident response plan** documented
5. **Default passwords changed** on ALL systems
6. **Network segmentation** - critical systems isolated from internet
7. **Backup procedures** - critical data backed up and tested
8. **Remote access control** - authorized, logged, supervised

### High-Priority Best Practices

9. Access control and password management
10. Malware protection (antivirus installed and updated)
11. Patch management procedures
12. Asset inventory and network diagram current
13. Physical security of critical systems
14. Drill program includes cyber scenarios
15. Monitoring and logging procedures

### Continuous Improvement

16. Annual risk reassessment
17. Procedure updates based on lessons learned
18. Threat intelligence monitoring
19. Information sharing with industry
20. Technology improvement planning
